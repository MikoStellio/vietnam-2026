Food Safety Rules (how to avoid food poisoning)

  1. Eat where the locals eat and it's busy. High turnover = fresh ingredients. A stall with a line of Vietnamese customers is safer than one waiting for tourists.
  2. Hot & cooked beats raw & cold. Choose dishes served piping hot (pho, grilled meat, fried rice). Skip raw salads and uncooked herbs unless from a clean shop — they're washed in tap water.
  3. No ice, no tap water. Drink sealed bottled water only. Say "không đá" (no ice) if you want to be safe. Ice at street stalls is often made from tap water.
  4. Watch the cook. Clean wok, food kept covered, meat cooked in front of you = good signs.
  5. Fruit: pick whole fruit you can peel yourself (banana, mango, orange). Avoid pre-cut fruit and fruit left out uncovered.
  6. Carry a backup kit: oral rehydration salts (ORS), anti-diarrheal wipes, and Imodium — buy at any local pharmacy ("nhà thuốc"). Use sparingly; ORS is the main thing if you get hit.
  7. Alcohol: stick to branded beer (Bia Hanoi, Saigon) in restaurants. Skip unbranded rice wine from random stalls.
Philippino-friendly baseline: Vietnamese street food is generally cleaner and hotter-cooked than PH street food. The biggest risks for you are (1) ice/tap water, (2) raw herbs/salads, (3) undercooked meat. Follow the rules above and you'll be fine.

Sapa · Nov 3–4

Sapa — Must-Eat Foods & Where to Eat Safely

Must-eat list (all safe if cooked hot)

  • Sapa rice wine (rượu gạo) — local specialty, mild and sweet. Try a small glass at dinner; skip it for kids.
  • Grilled chicken / pork with sticky rice (xôi + thịt nướng) — Sapa's signature street food, sold hot from grills in the night market.
  • Vietnamese hotpot (lẩu) — great family dinner: broth cooked at the table, so everything is freshly cooked = safest option.
  • Phở — you'll have it in Hanoi too; a Sapa bowl is fine but not special. Save your appetite for Hanoi phở.
  • Bánh tẻ / bánh cuốn (steamed rice rolls) — soft, mild, easy on the stomach; good breakfast if you don't want homestay food twice.
  • Chestnut cake & roasted chestnuts — Sapa's cold-weather snack from market stalls (freshly baked = safe).

Ninh Binh — Must-Eat Foods & Where to Eat Safely

Must-eat list (regional specialties)

  • Bún chả Tam Cốc / grilled pork with rice noodles — Ninh Binh's version of Hanoi's bún chả; local pride dish.
  • Cơm cháy (crispy fried rice crust) — the bottom layer of a clay-pot rice, fried until crackly. A beloved snack/dish in this region.
  • Xôi (sticky rice) with grilled chicken or pork — cheap, hot, filling; great after a long day of walking/cycling.
  • Chả cá / steamed fish in turmeric broth — fresh river fish, cooked at the table = safe and delicious.
  • Phở bò (beef noodle soup) — solid local bowls; cheaper than Hanoi's famous ones.

Hanoi · Nov 6–8

Hanoi — Must-Eat Foods (the authentic list)

  • Phở bò / phở gà — the national dish. Beef or chicken noodle soup, eaten hot.
  • Bún chả — grilled pork pattions + skewers over rice noodles in fish-sauce broth (Hanoi's true lunch icon).
  • Bánh mì — the Vietnamese baguette sandwich; grab one for breakfast or a light meal.
  • Bún bò Huế — spicier, lemongrass beef noodle soup from central Vietnam but excellent in Hanoi too.
  • Chả cá — turmeric-flavored fish pieces cooked at the table with dill and lime (Hanoi specialty).
  • Bánh cuốn — delicate steamed rice rolls, often with minced pork + wood-ear mushroom; a gentle, safe dish.
  • Nem rán (spring rolls) — fried pork-vegetable rolls with dipping sauce; order fresh-fried, not pre-made.
  • Chè / dessert — sweet soups (mung bean, coconut) or a slice of bánh flan; easy on the stomach after big meals.
  • Egg coffee (cà phê trứng) — must-try once; order it warm.
Avoid / be careful: raw herb salads and uncooked herbs on the side of phở (skip them if you're cautious), pre-cut fruit, ice in drinks ("không đá"), and any stall with no local customers. Everything above is safe when served hot from a busy place.

Souvenirs & Practical Notes

Good souvenirs (light + safe to bring home)

  • Coffee: Trung Nguyen or G7 instant coffee, or roasted beans — Vietnam's #1 souvenir.
  • Fish sauce (nước mắm) in a sealed bottle — check airline liquid rules for carry-on; checked bag is safer.
  • Dried fruits / snacks: mango chips, dried longan, coconut candy from the market.
  • Silk & handicrafts: silk scarves (Old Quarter), woven bags from Sapa night market.
  • Ceramics if you can carry them — but fragile; skip with kids in tow unless careful.

Practical notes for the whole trip

  • Money: withdraw VND from ATMs (fees apply) or exchange at banks/exchange offices — avoid airport rates. Most places take cash; some restaurants take cards.
  • Clothes for Sapa in November: cool mornings/evenings (~10–15 °C), cooler on Fansipan summit (5–10 °C). Bring a warm jacket + closed shoes.
  • Sleeper bus (Sapa → Ninh Binh, 9 hrs): bring snacks, a neck pillow, and download offline maps — the route passes through mountains with patchy signal.
  • Pharmacy kit: ORS sachets, Imodium, band-aids, any personal meds. Any pharmacy ("nhà thuốc") stocks these cheaply.
  • Day 6 flight (8:25 AM): online check-in before you sleep; be at Noi Bai T2 by ~6 AM as planned — international flights close boarding early.
